Where the money leaks in paper-based campus accounting

An entitlement nobody can verify at the counter, a charge nobody can attribute afterwards, and a month-end total that balances only against itself. Paper and spreadsheet accounting tends to hide loss rather than prevent it. This note works through the specific points where value escapes, why the gap stays nearly invisible in aggregate figures, and what changes once every charge carries an identity, a timestamp and a cost center.

// Note 002Coming soon

What a campus credential actually has to prove

Every campus transaction is an identity claim, usually settled by a staff member recognizing a face or accepting a card. This note is about making that claim verifiable without slowing the queue: how quickly a credential should stop being valid, what has to happen the day someone loses it, why a shared device at a counter needs a different trust model from a phone in a pocket, and where the honest limits of all of it sit.

// Note 003Coming soon

What becomes automatable once the record is complete

Most campus automation stalls because the underlying data is scattered across spreadsheets, portals and inboxes. Once programs, attendance, allowances, payments and approvals share one structured record, a large class of repetitive administrative work becomes describable as a rule. This note maps which operations are worth automating first, which should stay deterministic rather than predictive, and where a human approval step is not optional.
